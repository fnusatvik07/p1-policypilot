Purpose

Use this page when a dispute or claim needs management attention, policy clarification, legal review, or a handoff outside normal processing. For standard processing steps, follow the applicable CPS-SOP in the Disputes Ops Library. As of 2023-06-05, disputes work is processed in ClaimTrack 2.0 with automated deadline clocks.

First question: what kind of escalation is it?

Situation Primary contact Use when
Claim needs workflow guidance Team lead or manager in the owning unit The associate is unsure which CPS-SOP to apply, or the file has an unusual fact pattern
Policy interpretation needed Priya Raman, SVP, Director of Disputes Operations The issue affects coverage, timelines, notices, or handling standards across multiple cases
Compliance or regulatory interpretation needed Dana Whitfield, Chief Compliance Officer or Samuel Okafor, Director, Regulatory Change Management A question may affect consumer compliance, regulatory change mapping, or required disclosures
QA or repeat error trend Ifeoma Adeyemi, VP, Disputes Quality Assurance A review identifies a systemic issue, control gap, or recurring documentation defect

Where to send specific issue types

  • Customer complaints about disputes: route under CPS-SOP-228 and coordinate with Complaint Management Policy (POL-003).
  • Credit card billing error issues: contact Hannah Brandt or consult CPS-SOP-209 and POL-009.
  • FlexCard prepaid disputes: contact Luis Carvajal for investigation routing and consult CPS-SOP-208 and POL-010.
  • Policy or procedure ownership questions: contact Priya Raman first; if the issue is policy-level, loop in Dana Whitfield or Samuel Okafor.
  • Documentation, retention, or evidence questions: consult CPS-SOP-229 and POL-004 before releasing or discarding records.
  • External complaint escalation: use CPS-SOP-235 for CFPB Portal complaints involving disputes.

Quick rules

  • Use the owning manager first for case-specific questions.
  • Use Priya Raman for anything that may affect multiple cases, multiple teams, or published procedure language.
  • Use Compliance when the issue touches customer notices, regulatory timing, liability, or error-resolution treatment.
  • Use Fraud Operations when the facts suggest compromise beyond a routine dispute.
  • Document the escalation in the case notes and include the reason for escalation, date, and owner contacted.
  • If the matter is time-sensitive and the correct owner is unclear, escalate without delay.
