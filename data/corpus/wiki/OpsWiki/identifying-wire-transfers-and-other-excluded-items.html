Identifying wire transfers and other excluded items

Created by Darnell Price · Last updated 2023-06-05 · Version 2

WI-008 — Identifying wire transfers and other excluded items

Type: Work instruction
System(s): ClaimTrack 2.0, CardView, Corvane Card Services portal
Owner: Darnell Price, Manager, Operations Training
Version: 2
Effective/issued date: 2023-06-05
Status at issue: Active

Purpose

Use this page to determine whether a customer-reported transaction is a wire transfer or another item excluded from the standard card or EFT dispute workflow. Accurate identification at intake prevents misclassification, avoids duplicate handling, and ensures the case is routed to the correct CPS-SOP procedure. This version reflects the ClaimTrack 2.0 workflow and automated deadline clocks.

Before you start

Before you start - Open the claim in ClaimTrack 2.0 and confirm the transaction date, amount, and funding source. - Review CardView for card-present, card-not-present, and processor notes before assigning a dispute type. - If the item appears on a deposit account statement or wire advice, check HLX Core and the Corvane Card Services portal before proceeding. - Do not classify an excluded item as a card dispute solely because the customer used a debit card or enrolled digital channel to initiate the activity.

Procedure

  1. Open the claim in ClaimTrack 2.0.
    From the Claims Work Queue, search by claim number, customer name, or masked account number. Open the case summary and review the transaction description in the Claim Details panel.

  2. Review the automated clocks before you classify the item.
    In the top banner, confirm the case shows the ClaimTrack 2.0 deadline clocks. If the case is already assigned a category, note the current status before making changes. Do not rely on the clock alone to determine case type.

  3. Confirm the transaction source in ClaimTrack.
    Select Transaction History and open the posted item. Verify whether the transaction was posted through:

  4. Card network activity,
  5. ACH,
  6. Wire transfer activity,
  7. Deposit account transfer activity, or

  8. Another payment channel.

  9. Check CardView for card association.
    Open CardView in a second window and search the customer’s card profile. Review the recent activity list and issuer notes. If the item is linked to a card authorization, settlement, or chargeback lifecycle, continue with the applicable card procedure. If the item is labeled as wire-related or deposit-transfer-related, continue to Step 5.

  10. Open the Corvane Card Services portal.
    In Corvane, search using the customer’s card reference and transaction date. Review the activity screen and confirm whether Corvane shows:

  11. Network chargeback activity,
  12. Refund or reversal activity,
  13. Settlement adjustment, or

  14. No corresponding card record.

  15. Identify wire transfers.
    If the transaction is a wire transfer, document the wire indicators shown in ClaimTrack, CardView, Corvane, or the supporting document. Typical indicators include:

  16. Wire reference number,
  17. Wire advice or confirmation,
  18. Incoming or outgoing wire wording,

  19. Beneficiary or originator details associated with a wire instruction.

  20. Identify other excluded items.
    Review the claim narrative and supporting documents for items outside the standard card dispute path, including:

  21. Internal account-to-account transfers,
  22. Recurring bill payment instructions,
  23. Cash withdrawal activity,
  24. Branch-assisted transfer instructions,

  25. Non-card network payment instructions that do not appear in CardView or Corvane.

  26. Set the case classification in ClaimTrack 2.0.
    Select Edit Case and update the dispute category to match the item type. Use the classification fields to distinguish card, EFT, wire, and other non-card items. Add a short case note describing the evidence reviewed and why the item is excluded from the standard card workflow.

  27. Route the case to the correct queue.
    If the item is a wire transfer or other excluded item, select the appropriate non-card queue from the Routing menu under the intake guidance. If the item remains unclear, leave the case in the review state and escalate to the lead before sending it forward.

  28. Document the determination.
    Record the screen names reviewed, the indicators found, and the final classification in ClaimTrack 2.0. Keep notes brief, factual, and consistent with the transaction record.

Tip
A transaction may look like a card payment on the customer’s statement but still be a wire transfer or other excluded item once you review the processor and deposit-system records.

Warning
Do not use merchant names alone to determine case type. Some wire and transfer services use merchant-like descriptors that can be misleading without review in CardView, Corvane, and HLX Core.

Note
If the customer reports multiple transactions, classify each item separately. One claim may contain both card disputes and excluded items.
