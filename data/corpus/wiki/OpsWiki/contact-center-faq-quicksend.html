Purpose

Use this job aid when assisting customers with Harborline QuickSend in the mobile app. QuickSend is a person-to-person transfer service and is handled differently from debit card, ACH, and credit card disputes. See DOP-207 — QuickSend Person-to-Person Payment Disputes and TERMS-QS — Harborline QuickSend Service Terms.

Quick answers

Question Answer
Can a customer dispute a QuickSend transfer they sent? QuickSend payments initiated by the customer are generally final. Log the contact and follow the QuickSend review path if the customer is asking for reimbursement consideration.
What if the customer says the transfer was sent to the wrong person? Explain that QuickSend transfers initiated by the customer are not treated as unauthorized transfers solely because the recipient was incorrect. Log the matter for review under the Scam Reimbursement Guideline when applicable.
What if the customer believes someone accessed their app and sent a QuickSend transfer without permission? If a fraudster obtained the customer's QuickSend login credentials or one-time passcode and initiated the payment, the payment is treated as an unauthorized transfer under Regulation E, even if the customer was tricked into sharing the credentials.
What if the customer says they personally sent the payment after being persuaded by a scammer? If the customer personally sent a QuickSend payment to a scammer, the payment is not an unauthorized transfer, but the claim is still logged and reviewed under the Scam Reimbursement Guideline.
Which policy governs the handling path? Use POL-002 — Unauthorized Transfer Liability Policy, POL-001 — Consumer Error Resolution Policy (Electronic Funds Transfers and Card Disputes), and DOP-207 for classification and customer notice handling.

Handling steps for associates

  1. Confirm the product. Verify the customer is asking about QuickSend, not a debit card transaction, ACH debit, wire transfer, or bill payment.
  2. Clarify how the payment was made. Determine whether the customer:
  3. personally authorized and sent the payment,
  4. shared a login credential or one-time passcode and did not initiate the payment, or
  5. is reporting a scam or impersonation event.
  6. Classify correctly. Use the customer’s description to route the contact as an unauthorized transfer review or a scam reimbursement review.
  7. Document the facts. Record the date, amount, recipient information, customer explanation, and any statement about credential sharing or device access.
  8. Do not promise reversal. Advise the customer that the matter will be reviewed under the applicable policy and process.

Common call language

  • “I can review what happened and log the claim for the correct review path.”
  • “If someone used your login credentials or one-time passcode without your permission, we treat that differently from a payment you personally sent.”
  • “If you sent the payment yourself, I can still log the claim for review under the Scam Reimbursement Guideline.”

Do not

  • Treat a customer-sent QuickSend payment as an unauthorized transfer.
  • Classify QuickSend as a debit card purchase, ACH debit, or credit card billing error.
  • Advise that QuickSend has chargeback rights.
