Purpose

Quick reference for dispute-processing timing in Disputes Operations. Use this job aid with DOP-201, DOP-210, DOP-211, DOP-212, and DOP-231. This version reflects the IA-2021-07 remediation update and supersedes prior timing guidance where the procedures were revised.

Core timing rules

  • A customer's notice of error is timely if Harborline receives it no later than 60 days after we sent the periodic statement on which the error first appeared.
  • Harborline must investigate and determine whether an error occurred within 10 business days of receiving the notice of error.
  • If the investigation cannot be completed within 10 business days, Harborline may take up to 45 calendar days, provided that it provisionally credits the account for the amount in dispute within 10 business days of receiving the notice.
  • The 45-day period is extended to 90 calendar days if the transfer was not initiated within a state, resulted from a point-of-sale debit card transaction, or occurred within 30 days after the first deposit to the account was made.
  • For a transfer that occurred within 30 days after the first deposit to the account was made, the initial investigation period is 20 business days instead of 10.
  • Harborline's internal target is to decide and post provisional credit by the 7th business day after the notice of error.

At-a-glance cheat sheet

Quick FAQ

Q: What do I check first?
A: Receipt date, periodic statement date, transaction type, and whether the claim is a new-account transfer.

Q: Where do I route the case?
A: Route by dispute type: - Debit / EFT / ATM / ACH / QuickSend: Claims Intake → Debit & EFT Investigations - Credit card billing errors: Claims Intake → Credit Card Investigations - Chargebacks: Claims Intake → Chargeback Recovery - Fraud indicators: Refer under the fraud workflow before continuing

Q: What if the customer contacts a branch or the contact center?
A: Create or update the dispute case using the applicable channel procedure and preserve the original contact details. See DOP-224 and DOP-225.

Q: What if the claim is incomplete?
A: Document the missing information, follow the intake procedure, and do not guess on dates or amounts.

Common “do not miss” items

  • Confirm the statement date before you calculate the 60-day notice window.
  • Use the correct business-day calendar under DOP-231.
  • Set the provisional credit diary date at intake, then verify it again after classification.
  • If multiple transactions are involved, document each item separately.
  • Escalate timing exceptions through the supervisor queue in Claims Intake or the assigned investigations team.
