WI-025 — QuickSend claims: scam vs unauthorized

Type: Work Instruction
Status at issue: Active
Version: 1
Effective / issued: 2020-05-01
Owner: Darnell Price, Manager, Operations Training

This page explains how Claims Intake associates classify a Harborline QuickSend claim as scam or unauthorized in ClaimTrack, then route the case for the correct handling path. Use this work instruction together with DOP-207, DOP-203, DOP-210, and the customer terms in TERMS-QS. This page is limited to QuickSend person-to-person payment claims opened through OpsWiki-driven intake workflows.

[!NOTE] Before you start - Confirm the customer is reporting a QuickSend transfer and not a debit card purchase, ACH debit, wire transfer, or bill pay item. - Open the claim in ClaimTrack before making a scam vs unauthorized classification. - Review the transfer details in CardView and, when needed, the QuickSend account activity in the Corvane portal. - If the customer says they did authorize the transfer but was tricked, treat the report as a possible scam claim. - If the customer says they did not authorize the transfer at all, treat the report as a possible unauthorized claim.

1. Open the claim in ClaimTrack

  1. Sign in to ClaimTrack.
  2. Open Search Case.
  3. Search by customer name, mobile number, or existing claim number.
  4. Open the correct case from the search results.
  5. In the Claim Type field, select QuickSend.
  6. In the Issue Category field, select the closest matching QuickSend issue.
  7. Review the customer statement and note whether the transfer was:
  8. knowingly approved under false pretenses, or
  9. not approved by the customer at all.

[!TIP] Use the customer’s exact language in the claim notes. If the customer says “I sent it because I was told to,” that usually supports scam review. If the customer says “I never sent it,” that usually supports unauthorized review.

2. Check transfer details in CardView

  1. Open CardView in a new session.
  2. Search for the customer’s linked debit card or deposit account.
  3. Review the QuickSend transaction history.
  4. Confirm the transfer date, amount, and recipient reference.
  5. Compare the CardView activity with the statement recorded in ClaimTrack.
  6. If the customer’s description and the system record conflict, document the mismatch in the case notes before proceeding.

[!WARNING] Do not reclassify a case based only on the customer’s frustration, urgency, or complaint tone. Classification must be based on the reported facts and the transaction record.

3. Validate the QuickSend details in Corvane

  1. Open the Corvane portal.
  2. Locate the payment event using the date, amount, and customer account reference.
  3. Review the transfer status and any available recipient metadata.
  4. Check whether the payment completed normally or shows an exception marker.
  5. If Corvane data supports a completed customer-initiated transfer, continue the scam path.
  6. If Corvane data suggests the customer did not initiate the transfer, continue the unauthorized path.

4. Classify the claim

  1. In ClaimTrack, open the Classification tab.
  2. Select Scam when the customer was induced to send the QuickSend payment to a third party through deception, impersonation, or false pretenses.
  3. Select Unauthorized when the customer states the QuickSend payment was not initiated or approved by the customer.
  4. Add a concise note that states:
  5. what the customer reported,
  6. what CardView showed,
  7. what Corvane showed, and
  8. why the final classification was chosen.
  9. Save the case.

5. Route the claim

  1. Use the ClaimTrack workflow action to route the case to the next queue based on the selected classification.
  2. Verify the queue name before submitting.
  3. If the case has incomplete facts, pause and request clarification through the approved customer contact workflow in ClaimTrack.
  4. If the case includes indicators of broader account compromise, follow the escalation path in DOP-218.

[!NOTE] Scam and unauthorized are different handling paths. Do not use them interchangeably, even when the customer says “fraud” or “scammed.” Apply the claim facts, not the customer shorthand.
