Purpose

Quick reference for dispute-processing time limits and routing expectations in Disputes Operations. Use this job aid with the applicable procedures in the DOP series, especially DOP-201, DOP-210, DOP-211, DOP-212, and DOP-229.

Core timing rule

Customers must report a disputed transaction within 60 days of the transaction date.

At-a-glance cheat sheet

Topic What to remember Primary reference
Intake timing Log the claim as soon as it is received and document the channel, date received, and transaction date. DOP-201
Reporting window Compare the customer’s report date to the transaction date before routing the case. DOP-201, DOP-203

FAQ

Q: What do I check first?
A: Transaction date, report date, claim type, and channel. Then confirm whether the claim belongs in debit, ATM, ACH, credit card, or chargeback handling.

Q: Where do I route the case?
A: Route by dispute type: - Debit / EFT / ATM / ACH: Claims Intake → Debit & EFT Investigations - Credit card billing errors: Claims Intake → Credit Card Investigations - Merchant chargebacks: Claims Intake → Chargeback Recovery - Fraud indicators: Refer per fraud workflow before proceeding

Q: What if the customer contacts a branch or the contact center?
A: Create or update the dispute case using the applicable channel procedure and preserve the original contact details. See DOP-224 and DOP-225.

Q: What if the claim is incomplete?
A: Document the missing information, follow the intake procedure, and do not guess on dates or amounts.

Common “do not miss” items

  • Confirm the transaction date before applying any deadline test.
  • Use the same claim ID across all follow-up activity.
  • If multiple transactions are involved, document each item separately.
  • Retain supporting documents, screenshots, and notes in the case file.
  • Escalate unusual or high-value items through the applicable approval path.
